Adoption checks widget

A checklist widget: three adoption checks, each a ratio, with a verdict in the corner. The content is useful and the rows are the right shape; the question is how much table furniture three rows need, and where the verdict and the gaps should live.

What is there now

Reproduced for comparison, with the issues it carries.

  • Status column repeats the result. "1 of 2" and "1 gap" are the same fact; so are "1 of 9" and "8 gaps". A whole column does the subtraction the reader already did.
  • Table furniture for three rows. Uppercase column headers and a header rule for a three-item checklist. The labels name themselves; the headers add a row and nothing else.
  • Verdict as dot and text. "Low adoption" is a status word, so it is a tinted pill, not a dot beside plain text.
  • Same red for 50% and 11%. Both failing checks get an identical dot. One is half done, the other barely started; nothing on the card shows which.
  • Not applicable at full weight. A dash and a grey dot take a complete row with a rule. It is a footnote, not a finding.
  • No action. Nine gaps and nowhere to go. A checklist that finds gaps should offer the one link that closes them.
  • No scope. Which account or workspace, and as of when, is not said.

A · List, tidied

Closest to the original. The column headers go, the verdict becomes a pill in the title, and the gap counts become pills so the status column is no longer prose with a dot.

  • The not-applicable row drops to a caption that says why it does not apply.
  • One action: "View devices", where both gaps are closed.
  • Trade-off: result and gap still say the same thing twice, and 50% and 11% still look alike.

B · Check rows with meters Recommended

The list stays a list, and each row gets the hairline meter we use for drivers. The meter shows how far each check is from passing; the pill names the gap.

  • Magnitude is visible: 50% is amber and half full, 11% is red and nearly empty. The tint follows the value, not a blanket red.
  • The gap pill earns its place because it is the thing to act on; the ratio is the evidence beside it.
  • Not applicable keeps its row for completeness, in soft ink with an empty track, so the checklist is still three checks long.
  • Trade-off: four columns need about 480px of width; in a half-width card the pill wraps under the value.

C · Multi-stat

The two live checks become stats in a W2 strip: rate as the headline, count as the unit, gap as the pill, meter beneath. The same shape as the user engagement widget.

  • Strongest at a glance; the numbers are at headline size and the meters sit side by side.
  • Trade-off: it stops being a checklist. Adding a fourth or fifth check means another column, where B just adds a row.
Recommendation

Go with B. This widget is a checklist, and a checklist grows by rows, so the list is the right shape; it just needed the furniture removed and a mark added. The title carries the verdict as a pill, each check has a meter tinted by how far it is from passing, the gap is a pill the reader can act on, and a single "View devices" link is where the gaps get closed.

Whichever wins: the column headers go, the verdict is a pill not a dot, no column repeats another, the two failing checks look as different as 50% and 11% are, not applicable drops to soft ink or a caption, and the card has a scope line and one action.
