Peer benchmarking widget

An account metric compared against its sector. The content is right; the question is how it maps onto the widget anatomy we already use: title and scope, one value row, one mark, a caption and a single action.

What is there now

Reproduced for comparison, with the issues it carries.

Industry: Technology and Digital · Sector: Software and SaaS

Ever-logged-in rate = invited users who have logged in at least once ÷ invited users (Activation benchmarking pull; not the 30-day active count). Peer status = within sector Mean ± 1 SD; rank is within the same Google-tier sector.
  • No headline. The answer, 60.4%, sits in the third column of a bar row at body size. Nothing on the card is the value row.
  • Title outside the frame. An uppercase section label above the card instead of a widget title inside it. The metric name only appears mid-sentence.
  • Labels as prose. "Industry: X · Sector: Y" and "status: pill · Rank #2 of 4" are two sentences doing the job of a scope line and a caption.
  • Empty bars. Both tracks are grey with no visible fill, so 60.4% and 32.8% look identical.
  • Colour in type. "+27.6 pts" is green text and "On track" is amber text. The rule is ink on a tinted pill.
  • Status colour mismatch. "On track" on an amber pill reads as a warning.
  • Method as footnote. Two lines of definition inside the card; that belongs behind "How is this calculated?".
  • Full width, third used. The card spans the page while the content stops at 600px.

Closest to the original. The two bars stay, but filled, with a value row above them and the labels moved into their slots.

  • Title carries the metric and the status pill; scope line carries industry and sector.
  • Delta becomes the top line of the value row, in a tinted pill.
  • Trade-off: the headline and the first bar say 60.4% twice.

B · Meter with benchmark marker Recommended

One mark. The account is the fill; the sector average is a reference tick, named in the inline legend the same way a dashed prior-period line is on a sparkline.

  • Nothing is said twice: 60.4% is the headline, the bar shows how far above the tick it lands.
  • Fits the M2 meter spec already in the Visualisation page, so it sits beside health meters without a new pattern.
  • Trade-off: the sector value is in a legend, not a bold figure. Fine for a benchmark, where the gap is the point.

C · Multi-stat, no mark

Three value rows in a W2 strip: account, sector, rank. For readers who want the numbers, not the picture.

  • Rank gets equal billing, which the original buried in a sentence.
  • Trade-off: no sense of scale; 60.4 against 32.8 is a mental subtraction rather than a glance.
Recommendation

Go with B. It is the existing M2 meter with a reference marker, so the card looks like every other metric widget: title with status pill, scope line, value row with the delta as a pill, one mark, a one-line caption and a single "How is this calculated?" action holding the method text.

Whichever wins: the section label moves inside as the title, the definition leaves the card, bars get a visible fill, the status pill is green when the status is good, and the card is sized to its content rather than the page.
