Account health and snapshot

Two side-by-side widgets on an account page: a health score with its drivers, and a snapshot of account facts. The question is what each one is for, what they repeat, and how the drivers should be shown.

  • Repetition across frames. "1 of 9 devices active" appears in both widgets, and "8 not active" is the same fact a third time.
  • Repetition within a row. "2 paid subscriptions" then "Paid"; "2026-10-27" then "1 day ago". Each pair says one thing twice.
  • Status dots on non-statuses. A green dot beside "Paid" and "1 day ago" borrows health semantics for facts that are not health.
  • No mark. The score is a number and a word. A 0 to 100 score is the textbook case for a meter, which would also replace the "Neutral" line.
  • Drivers as prose. Three factors are three ratios, but they are written as sentences with no sense of which hurts most.
  • Mixed scales. A count, a ratio and an ISO date share one 30px type size. A date is not a headline number.
  • Uneven height. The left card ends early and leaves a block of empty space the right card does not.

A · Tidy both in place

Same two jobs, same two frames. The score gets an M2 meter with the status as a pill in the delta slot; the drivers become ratio rows with a mini meter each. The snapshot becomes a W2 multi-stat strip.

  • Every value row uses the locked stack, so the two cards line up.
  • Driver rows sort worst first, and the meter fill carries severity.
  • Trade-off: devices still appear in both frames, and the date drops to a caption.

B · Two widgets, two distinct jobs Recommended

Health owns everything that moves the score. Account owns the facts that do not: plan, enrolment, last login. Nothing appears twice.

  • Account keeps one headline (subscriptions) and a key-facts list; dates and plan names stop pretending to be metrics.
  • "Devices active" lives only in Health, where its ratio explains the score. The snapshot keeps the flat count, 9 enrolled.
  • No status dots on facts; the only colour is in the meters and the status pill.
  • Trade-off: the snapshot loses its big-number feel. That is the point, but it reads quieter.

C · One widget

The snapshot folds into Health: counts move to the scope line, the remaining facts to a meta row at the foot. One frame, one subject.

  • Most compact; the empty-space problem disappears.
  • Trade-off: plan and login facts are now tucked under a health heading where nobody will look for them, and the widget has no room to grow.
  • Use when the page has a separate account details section elsewhere.

D5 · BadgesEach driver as one tinted chip holding label and value, wrapping as space allows. Severity in the tint, worst first. Most compact, but the chips look clickable and long labels must be cut, so "last 30 days" goes to the tooltip.

Recommendation

Go with B. The pair earns two frames once each has a different job: Health explains a score, Account states facts. Removing the duplicated device ratio and the doubled-up status lines fixes most of what is wrong, and the key-facts list stops dates being set at headline size.

For the drivers, D2 bar list: it keeps full labels, sorts worst first, and ties each bar to the score meter above it. Use D3 when each driver has an action behind it. If bars are out, D4 plain text over D5 badges: it keeps full labels and does not look like a row of buttons.

Whichever wins: the score gets a meter, the drivers sort worst first with colour in the fill or pill only, status words live in a pill not a dot-plus-text line, and the "How is this calculated?" link stays as the widget's single action.
