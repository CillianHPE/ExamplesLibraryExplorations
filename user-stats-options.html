User stat cards

Four stat cards about one population of users. The frames are consistent and the type is right; the question is what each card is for, which slots are filled with the wrong thing, and whether four frames are needed.

  • Population as a card. "114 users scored" is the denominator for the other three; as a peer card it says "114" once, then "of 114" again in the last card.
  • Zero said twice. "Power users: 0.0% of users" and "0". A zero headline is a non-event; it wants a quiet statement, not a 30px number.
  • The right slot, wrong content. "0.0% of users" and "vs ~28% platform-wide" are a unit and a delta sitting in the scope line. The value row has slots for both.
  • Comparison without a direction. 10 of 114 is 8.8%, against 28% platform-wide: 19 points behind. The card makes the reader do the arithmetic and never says which way it points.
  • Percent floating. "87 %" with a space; and 87% of what is never stated (99 of 114 users).
  • No marks, no status. 87% at risk and 0 power users are alarming. Nothing on the strip signals it; four identical grey cards read as neutral.
  • Label grammar. "User scored" should be "Users scored"; "At-risk" is hyphenated here and not elsewhere.

C · Two widgets by question

"How engaged are users?" and "Are they tracked in Amplitude data?" are different questions, so they get a frame each. Engagement becomes a proportion bar; the zero count drops to a caption.

  • Engagement gains a picture: 99 against 15 is visible, not read.
  • Amplitude gets the same benchmark meter as the peer benchmarking widget.
  • Trade-off: power users lose their own number. Right while it is zero; revisit once it is not.

  • Every card says its subtitle twice. "Across 15 workspaces" sits above the number and again below it; so do "0.0% of users" and "vs ~28% platform-wide". The footer is the scope line with a bold word.
  • Three things compete for the eye. A bold title, a 30px number and a bold footer fragment. A stat card has one headline; everything else is quieter than it.
  • The footer carries the unit. "87.7% of users" and "of 114" are units; "vs 28%" is a delta. They belong in the value row beside the number, not in a sentence below it.
  • Numbers disagree. The headline says 87%, the footer says 87.7%. One rounding per card.
  • Noise in the figures. "0.0%" puts a decimal on nothing; "~28%" puts an approximation mark in a UI; "vs" is an abbreviation where "below" says the direction.
  • Still there from the first pass. "User scored", a population shown as a peer card, and no status on 87%.

Recommendation

Go with B. It fixes every slot error (units and deltas in the value row, population in the scope line), gives each ratio a meter so 87% looks as bad as it is, and uses one frame for one subject. Move to C when the page has room for a proportion bar and the engagement split matters more than the individual counts.

Whichever wins: the denominator is said once, zero gets a "None yet" pill rather than a headline, every ratio shows a mark, comparisons state direction and size, and the scope line describes the population rather than restating the number.
