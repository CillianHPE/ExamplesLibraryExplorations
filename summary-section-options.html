Summary section

Most overview pages open with a few lines that say what matters before the widgets do. This looks at how other products build that section, what the current one does, and four shapes it could take: a digest, a findings list, recommendation cards, or a banner with a rail.

What is consistent

  • A short bold lead per item, a grey reason after it.
  • Bold figures inside sentences; no colour in the text itself.
  • Counts in the title ("4", "13") so the reader knows the size before reading.
  • One action per item, right-aligned or inline at the end.

Where they differ

  • Severity: analytics tools mark good or bad with an icon; security tools use tinted pills and sort by it.
  • Dismissal: content that the user can act on is dismissible per item; content that describes state is not.
  • Frame: setup lists are contained cards; AI digests sit on the body with a hairline.

Rules this suggests

  • Dismiss the item, not the section, unless every item is optional.
  • Never dismissible: anything critical. It leaves when fixed.
  • Dismissed items go somewhere: "Show dismissed" or a count in the title.
  • Say when it was generated and from what.

The screens behind the table, grouped by pattern. Each caption says what to look at.

What is there now

Reproduced for comparison, with the issues it carries.

Summary✕ Dismiss
  • 9 out of 15 workspaces with no paid subscription; no subscriptions due for renewal in the next 90 days.
  • 15 workspaces with security gaps (MFA/SSO/IP).
  • Start with ALPHA BANK and Optima Bank today.
  • Plain bullets. Three sentences with no severity, order or mark. The security line, which is every workspace, looks the same as the subscription line.
  • The action is a sentence. "Start with ALPHA BANK and Optima Bank today" is bold text, not a link. Nothing on the section can be clicked except Dismiss.
  • Dismiss hides the lot. One X removes a critical finding along with a suggestion. And once gone, there is no way back.
  • Two facts per line. "No paid subscription; no renewals in 90 days" are separate findings joined by a semicolon.
  • "15 workspaces" is all of them. The sentence hides that the gap is universal; "All 15" says it.
  • Abbreviations and caps. "MFA/SSO/IP" and "ALPHA BANK" are system strings shown as prose.
  • Heading size. "Summary" is set as an H2 on the page; it competes with "Account overview" rather than sitting under it.
  • No scope or time. Nothing says what it was generated from or when.

S2 · Findings list Recommended

The security-tool shape, in our status order. A pill ranks each row, a bold lead names it, a soft line gives the reason, and the action sits in the same column on every row.

  • Count in the title; the reader knows it is three items before reading.
  • Dismissal is per row, from the overflow menu, and only for Warning and Suggested. Critical has no menu; it leaves when it is fixed.
  • "Show dismissed (1)" replaces the section-level X, so nothing is ever lost.
  • Uncontained, with hairlines, like a table on the page body. In a dashboard column it takes the grey card.
  • Trade-off: needs about 640px; below that the action wraps under the text.

S3 · Recommendation cards

The Google Workspace and Peec AI shape: one card per item, each with its own verdict controls.

  • Best when items are tasks the user completes: "Mark as done" makes sense of a card in a way it does not of a sentence.
  • Per-card X; the Critical card has none.
  • Trade-off: three cards take the height of a stat strip, and with four or more items it becomes a carousel. Heavy for a page that already has a widget grid below.

S4 · Banner and rail

The one critical item becomes a callout at the top of the body; the rest moves to the secondary rail under "Recommendations", in the order the layout rules already set: interrupt callout, quick links, recommendations, ambient metrics.

  • Strongest hierarchy: one thing interrupts, everything else waits at the side.
  • The banner is not dismissible while the condition holds; rail items dismiss individually.
  • Trade-off: only works on pages with a rail, and only when there is a single critical item. Two banners stack into noise.
Scenarios

The same three shapes, each in three jobs. The shape is the container; the job decides what goes in it, how it is ordered, and whether anything can be dismissed.

S1 · Digest

Sentences. For reading, not working through. Nothing in a digest is dismissible, because it describes state rather than asking for anything.

S1a · Status digest

Job: "Tell me how things stand right now." Three or four lines that say what is fine and what is not, so the reader can decide whether to read the rest of the page.

Use when
Someone lands on an overview after a gap and needs the state of play before the widgets. Mixed news: some good, some not.
Not when
Something is on fire. A critical finding wants a pill and a row (S2), not a sentence between two calm ones.
Shape
Status dot, bold subject, plain predicate. A link only on the lines that have somewhere to go. Good news gets no link.

S1b · Change digest

Job: "What happened while I was away?" Only deltas since a fixed point: last sign-in, last week, last release. Steady state is left out.

Use when
Returning users, weekly reviews, release notes for an account. Pages people visit on a cadence rather than live.
Not when
First visit or very frequent visits; there is nothing to compare against, or the list is empty most of the time.
Shape
Trend icon in the ink colour, bold quantity, what and where. A footer names the comparison point and links to the full activity log.

S1c · Generated narrative

Job: "Explain this data to me." Prose that interprets the numbers below it: trends, concentrations, causes. It adds meaning; it does not list findings.

Use when
Analytics and usage pages where the charts need a reader's commentary, and the product has a model behind it.
Not when
Compliance, security or billing, where a sentence that is slightly wrong does damage. Anything a user must act on today.
Shape
Beta tag, time generated, two short paragraphs with bold figures, follow-up prompts, a disclaimer and feedback. Regenerate rather than hide.

Rows with a pill and one action each. For working through. What the pill says, and how the rows are sorted, is the whole difference between these three.

Job: "What runs out, and when?" Things with a date on them, sorted by the date, so the reader sees how much runway there is.

Subscriptions, licences, certificates, trials, contracts. Pages where time, not severity, is the ranking.
Not when
The items have no date, or they all share one. If nothing is inside 90 days, show the count and nothing else.

S2c · Progress checklist

Job: "What is left to finish?" A fixed set of steps toward a known end state, with the finished ones ticked so the count climbs.

Use when
Onboarding, rollouts, migrations. Any time the list is the same for everyone and ends. Finished items stay visible until the whole thing is done.
Not when
The set is open-ended or keeps refilling; that is a triage list. Also not for optional improvements, which are S3b.
Shape
Tick or empty circle instead of a pill, "3 of 5" in the title, a scope line that says what done means. The section leaves on its own once complete.

S3a · Action cards

Job: "Do these things." A short, ranked set of tasks that must happen, each with its consequence spelled out and one button to start.

Use when
Three or fewer must-do items with a clear owner, on a page the owner visits. Each card is a task, not a fact.
Not when
More than three; it becomes a carousel. Or when the items are findings to read rather than tasks to finish, which is S2a.
Shape
Severity pill, imperative title ("Renew…", "Enforce…"), consequence in the body, one link. Critical cards have no X and no "Mark as done".

S3b · Opportunity cards

Job: "Here is what you could improve, if you want." Optional suggestions with a stated benefit and effort, which the reader is free to decline.

Use when
There is a recommendation engine, nothing is urgent, and the reader has slack. Settings, billing and workspace overview pages.
Not when
Anything that must happen; optional framing lets it be ignored. Or when you cannot say the benefit in one line.

S3c · Getting-started steps

Job: "Set up the next thing." A fixed sequence for a new account, with one step live at a time and the others shown so the reader knows how long the road is.

Use when
First weeks of an account, or after a big new capability ships. The user is unfamiliar and the order matters.
Not when
The account is established; replace with S2c if steps remain, or remove entirely. Never on a page that already has a primary button.
Shape
Numbered steps, the current one outlined with the page's single primary button, done steps ticked, future steps with a preview link. "Skip for now" hides the whole thing.

Picking one

  • Is the reader here to read or to work? Read: S1. Work: S2 or S3.
  • Are the items facts or tasks? Facts: S2. Tasks: S3.
  • Does the list end? Yes: S2c or S3c. No: S2a, S2b, S3a, S3b.

What never changes

  • Title names the job ("Needs attention", "Coming up"), not the shape ("Summary").
  • Count in the title when the items can be counted.
  • Scope line says what was checked and when.
  • One action per item; the subject leads every line.

Mixing

  • One summary section per page. If two jobs compete, the critical one wins the top slot and the other moves to the rail.
  • A digest (S1) can sit above a findings list (S2) only when the digest is one line.
Dismissal

What the research says about the X, condensed into rules.

Per item, not per section

A section X treats a critical finding and a suggestion the same. Dismiss rows or cards; offer "Hide" to collapse the section if it must go.

Critical is never dismissible

It has no menu and no X. It leaves the list when the underlying condition is fixed, and the title count drops.

Dismissed goes somewhere

"Show dismissed (n)" in the section header, or a count in the title. Dismissals persist per user and return if the finding worsens.

Recommendation

Go with S2. It gives the section the same anatomy as every other widget: title with a count, scope line, rows in status order with a pill, a lead, a reason and one action per row. Dismissal is per row and only where the item is optional, so the critical finding cannot vanish. Use S4 instead when a page has a rail and exactly one critical item; use S3 when the items are tasks the user completes rather than findings they read.

Whichever wins: the subject leads each line, the action is a link not a sentence, all-of-something says "All", abbreviations are spelled out, the heading sits at widget size under the page title, and a scope line says what it was generated from and when.
