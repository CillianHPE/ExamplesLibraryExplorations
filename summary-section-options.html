Summary section

Most overview pages open with a few lines that say what matters before the widgets do. This looks at how other products build that section, what the current one does, and four shapes it could take: a digest, a findings list, recommendation cards, or a banner with a rail.

What is consistent

  • A short bold lead per item, a grey reason after it.
  • Bold figures inside sentences; no colour in the text itself.
  • Counts in the title ("4", "13") so the reader knows the size before reading.
  • One action per item, right-aligned or inline at the end.

Where they differ

  • Severity: analytics tools mark good or bad with an icon; security tools use tinted pills and sort by it.
  • Dismissal: content that the user can act on is dismissible per item; content that describes state is not.
  • Frame: setup lists are contained cards; AI digests sit on the body with a hairline.

Rules this suggests

  • Dismiss the item, not the section, unless every item is optional.
  • Never dismissible: anything critical. It leaves when fixed.
  • Dismissed items go somewhere: "Show dismissed" or a count in the title.
  • Say when it was generated and from what.
What is there now

Reproduced for comparison, with the issues it carries.

  • 9 out of 15 workspaces with no paid subscription; no subscriptions due for renewal in the next 90 days.
  • 15 workspaces with security gaps (MFA/SSO/IP).
  • Start with ALPHA BANK and Optima Bank today.
  • Plain bullets. Three sentences with no severity, order or mark. The security line, which is every workspace, looks the same as the subscription line.
  • The action is a sentence. "Start with ALPHA BANK and Optima Bank today" is bold text, not a link. Nothing on the section can be clicked except Dismiss.
  • Dismiss hides the lot. One X removes a critical finding along with a suggestion. And once gone, there is no way back.
  • Two facts per line. "No paid subscription; no renewals in 90 days" are separate findings joined by a semicolon.
  • "15 workspaces" is all of them. The sentence hides that the gap is universal; "All 15" says it.
  • Abbreviations and caps. "MFA/SSO/IP" and "ALPHA BANK" are system strings shown as prose.
  • Heading size. "Summary" is set as an H2 on the page; it competes with "Account overview" rather than sitting under it.
  • No scope or time. Nothing says what it was generated from or when.

S1 · Digest

The Xero and Pipedrive shape. Still sentences, but each has a status dot, a bold subject and one link at the end. A footer says where it came from.

  • Lowest change from today: the same three lines, rewritten so the subject leads and the action is a link.
  • "Hide" collapses the section rather than deleting it; it is a description of state, so it is not dismissible.
  • Trade-off: prose is slow to scan. The status dot is the only thing that ranks the lines.

S2 · Findings list Recommended

The security-tool shape, in our status order. A pill ranks each row, a bold lead names it, a soft line gives the reason, and the action sits in the same column on every row.

  • Count in the title; the reader knows it is three items before reading.
  • Dismissal is per row, from the overflow menu, and only for Warning and Suggested. Critical has no menu; it leaves when it is fixed.
  • "Show dismissed (1)" replaces the section-level X, so nothing is ever lost.
  • Uncontained, with hairlines, like a table on the page body. In a dashboard column it takes the grey card.
  • Trade-off: needs about 640px; below that the action wraps under the text.

S3 · Recommendation cards

The Google Workspace and Peec AI shape: one card per item, each with its own verdict controls.

  • Best when items are tasks the user completes: "Mark as done" makes sense of a card in a way it does not of a sentence.
  • Per-card X; the Critical card has none.
  • Trade-off: three cards take the height of a stat strip, and with four or more items it becomes a carousel. Heavy for a page that already has a widget grid below.

S4 · Banner and rail

The one critical item becomes a callout at the top of the body; the rest moves to the secondary rail under "Recommendations", in the order the layout rules already set: interrupt callout, quick links, recommendations, ambient metrics.

  • Strongest hierarchy: one thing interrupts, everything else waits at the side.
  • The banner is not dismissible while the condition holds; rail items dismiss individually.
  • Trade-off: only works on pages with a rail, and only when there is a single critical item. Two banners stack into noise.
Dismissal

What the research says about the X, condensed into rules.

Per item, not per section

A section X treats a critical finding and a suggestion the same. Dismiss rows or cards; offer "Hide" to collapse the section if it must go.

Critical is never dismissible

It has no menu and no X. It leaves the list when the underlying condition is fixed, and the title count drops.

Dismissed goes somewhere

"Show dismissed (n)" in the section header, or a count in the title. Dismissals persist per user and return if the finding worsens.

Recommendation

Go with S2. It gives the section the same anatomy as every other widget: title with a count, scope line, rows in status order with a pill, a lead, a reason and one action per row. Dismissal is per row and only where the item is optional, so the critical finding cannot vanish. Use S4 instead when a page has a rail and exactly one critical item; use S3 when the items are tasks the user completes rather than findings they read.

Whichever wins: the subject leads each line, the action is a link not a sentence, all-of-something says "All", abbreviations are spelled out, the heading sits at widget size under the page title, and a scope line says what it was generated from and when.
