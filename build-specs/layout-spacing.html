Layout spacing scale

A page is built from three nested levels. Each level has a name, and the gap between siblings at each level has one token from the HPE design system spacing scale. Pick by relationship, not by eye: the further apart two things are in meaning, the bigger the token.

The three levels
Content area
The top-level split of the page body. Every view has a primary content area (the main work surface) and may have a secondary content area (a narrower rail for callouts, quick links and ambient metrics). There are never more than two.
Content section
A topic grouping inside a content area. A KPI strip, a chart row, a full-width table: each is one section. Sections stack vertically in the primary content area. The secondary area is small enough that it has no sections; cards sit in it directly.
Container / card
The individual modules inside a content section. Cards, tiles, chart containers. Grey containment signals a contained module; a table on the page body may be uncontained and full width.
The three tokens
xxlarge · 64px
Macro
Content area ↔ Content area
Between the primary and secondary content areas. The largest gap on any view; used exactly once.
large · 32px
Macro
Content section ↔ Content section
Between content sections within a content area: separates one topic from the next.
small · 16px
Meso
Card ↔ Card
Card sibling gap: between containers inside a content section (rows and columns), and between stacked cards in the secondary content area.

Rules of thumb: xxlarge appears exactly once per view, between the primary and secondary content areas. A content section's internal grid uses small for both row and column gaps. The secondary content area has no sections of its own, so its cards stack at small. Never mix sizes inside one grid, and never invent an in-between value: if a gap wants 24 px, the grouping is wrong, not the scale.
