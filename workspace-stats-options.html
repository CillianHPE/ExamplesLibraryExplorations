A strip of three cards reporting on one subject. The question is whether three containers are earning their keep, what the reader needs first, and what can be folded together.

  • Repetition. 15 is the sum of 2 + 3 + 10 + 0. The total card restates the breakdown card.
  • Three frames, one subject. Every card is about the same 15 workspaces, yet they read as three unrelated reports with no shared title.
  • Equal weight. Four numbers at the same size give no priority. The news is "10 of 15 at risk" and "score 34", not "15".
  • Zero count rendered. "Not yet scored 0" takes a quarter of the widest card to say nothing.
  • Colour in type. "34/100" in red; the rule is ink text with colour on a fill or pill.
  • Wordy labels. "Total number of workspaces" is "Workspaces" once the number is beside it.

A · Two widgets: score, then population

The total card is absorbed into the breakdown card as its headline. The score becomes an M2 meter so its colour sits in the fill, not the type.

  • Two questions, two answers: "how healthy?" and "how is it split?"
  • Status order worst first; zero count omitted.
  • Still two frames for one subject, and the number 15 appears twice (scope line and headline).

B · One widget, score as headline Recommended

One subject, one frame. The score is the answer; the proportion bar and legend are the evidence; the caption names what to do about it. Follows the Visualisation anatomy slot for slot.

  • Total moves to the scope line, where population belongs.
  • Every number is said once.
  • Hierarchy is explicit: 36px score, 13px counts, 12.5px caption.
  • Trade-off: the status counts lose their own big numbers. If someone scans for "how many at risk", the legend value is smaller than before.

C · One widget, multi-stat strip

Keeps big numbers for the counts that matter by turning the card into a W2 multi-stat. The bar beneath still shows the split; healthy drops to the caption because it is the non-event.

  • Fast scan for "how many at risk" with a trend on each.
  • Three value rows share the locked stack, so they line up.
  • Trade-off: more furniture than B, and the bar now partly repeats the numbers above it. Use when the counts are acted on individually.
Recommendation

Start with B. The strip exists to answer "are our workspaces healthy?" and the score answers it; the counts explain it. One container keeps the subject together and removes the duplicate total.

Move to C only if users act on the at-risk count directly (filtering, triage), where the count deserves headline size. Keep A for a dashboard where score and population have separate drill-down pages.

Whichever wins: zero counts stay out, status order is worst first, colour lives in the bar and pill, and the total lives in the scope line.
